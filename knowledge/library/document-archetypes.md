# Compose Documents Around the Reader's Task

Use this alongside document-design.md when creating or substantially reshaping native documents. These are content architectures, not mandatory skins. Preserve required legal structure, brand rules, supplied wording and explicit plain/minimal requests.

| Family | Opener | Continuation and Evidence | Avoid by Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal memo | Matter, recipient/date, issue and qualified conclusion in the required order | Stable numbered analysis, citations, chronology when relevant, readable paragraphs | Decorative counts of issues/events; dashboard-style metric strips |
| Executive brief | Decision or supported takeaway, scope/period and the few measures needed to judge it | Drivers, risks, options, evidence and accountable next action | Repeating the large opener and headline metrics on every page |
| Proposal | Client problem, intended outcome and offer | Scope, exclusions, milestones, price, assumptions and next step | Invented results/testimonials; key commercial qualifications in fine print |
| Editorial report | Subject-led opening, standfirst and an appropriate image or typographic gesture | Alternating narrative, evidence and chapter transitions; captions and source notes | A new visual gimmick per page; quotations invented to fill a sidebar |
| Handbook | Purpose, audience, navigation and how to use it | Consistent task-based headings, steps, warnings and examples | Identical shaded boxes for unrelated semantic roles |

## Apply the Architecture in the Renderer

The shipped Legal, Professional and Business examples bind these choices to both Word and HTML. The memo uses native metadata and quiet record counts, the brief places the decision before its measures, and the proposal uses offer terms and a delivery sequence. Their typography, table treatments and continuation-title scales differ. Preserve the underlying facts when changing representation. A new color palette applied to the same block sequence does not establish a new architecture.

## Design a Page Sequence

Distinguish the opener, continuation, evidence and closing roles. Repeat navigation and conventions; vary scale and density when the reading task changes. A continuation title can be quiet while preserving navigation. Design each page with actual content before fixing page breaks. A short memo need not acquire a cover or table of contents merely because a long-report template includes them.

Use a small set of native styles for title, subtitle, heading levels, body, caption and distinct callout roles. Keep direct formatting for intentional local emphasis. Define spacing, keep-with-next and table header behavior in reusable styles where supported. Use native lists and TOC fields when appropriate, verify their updates in the target editor, and keep essential text in logical reading order. Do not use tables solely to position the entire page.

## Give Charts an Editorial Job

For explanatory charts, use a supported takeaway as the title, with a statistical subtitle or caption identifying measure, period and units. Use a descriptive title for exploratory views or when the data cannot support a stronger conclusion. Label axes with actual meaning, not generic category/value placeholders. Preserve caveats and distinguish actuals from estimates. Provide useful alternatives and accessible values, not only a repeated title as alt text.

Prefer a readable static comparison or small multiples when interactivity adds no useful task. Use interaction for meaningful exploration, preserving a print/text equivalent. In Word, distinguish an editable native chart from an embedded image and retain its source values.

## Test Editing, Not Only Export

On a disposable copy, lengthen a representative title and paragraph, add table rows and inspect an available fallback font. Re-render affected pages and neighbors. Check stranded headings, row/header repetition, clipped labels, footer collisions, navigation and reading order. Record editor/version, font availability and tested cases. A LibreOffice render does not prove Microsoft Word fidelity; an accessible source does not by itself certify exported PDF tagging.

Gallery previews must come from the exact downloadable artifact. Show at least one useful interior page for multipage documents and retain source/capture provenance. Never call a guidance update a rebuilt or visually verified template.
