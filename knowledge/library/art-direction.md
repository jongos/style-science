# Make the Design Idea Visible

For new designs and substantial restyles, derive an original art direction from the prompt; examples are evidence, never automatic starting points. This is agent work, not a questionnaire or approval step. For small fixes, preserve the established direction. User wording, brand locks, language coverage and accessibility win.

For Word and PDF, use [the generative document workflow](document-design-space.md) to expand structures beyond the existing examples and verify native output.

## Dazzler's Creative Standard

Every output should have a strong, prompt-specific design voice. Push the expressive possibilities to the furthest coherent point allowed by the brief. Prefer an interesting, committed direction that may divide taste over an interchangeable compromise. Do not deliberately seek dislike: aim for memorable conviction, then respond to user feedback. Dazzler's recognizable signature is the quality and specificity of the decisions, not a recurring palette, logo, typeface or layout.

“Basic professional” narrows the vocabulary, not the ambition. Pursue exceptional proportion, decisive hierarchy, precise alignment, beautiful table typography and confident whitespace within the requested simplicity. A one-page monochrome memo can be as authored as an immersive site. Explicit plain-text, exact-template, accessibility, brand and format requirements remain real constraints; maximize craft in the remaining freedom. Small edits strengthen or preserve the existing voice within their scope.

## Design Principles

1. **Take a position.** Name the emotional register and connect it to the actual subject. “Modern, clean, premium” is not a concept. Specify what this design believes matters and make that priority visible.
2. **Make one move unmistakable.** Commit to a defining spatial, typographic, material, illustrative or interactive idea. Supporting decisions reinforce it. Pushing every element to maximum size, color and motion destroys contrast; push the overall idea through deliberate differences in intensity.
3. **Use type as composition.** Let real words shape proportion, alignment, scale and rhythm. Explore expressive axes, genuine italics, unusual scale relationships and editorial tension where supported. Keep reading text readable and verify actual files, glyphs and line breaks.
4. **Turn the subject into a visual rule.** Derive a diagram, sequence, motif, interaction or material treatment from the content. A river, orchestra and legal chronology should not merely receive different colors on the same skeleton.
5. **Choreograph density and release.** Compose packed versus open areas, long versus short beats, and surprising transitions. Design the entire page or document sequence, including ordinary tables and continuation pages. A spectacular cover does not rescue generic interiors.
6. **Make surfaces intentional.** Explore luminous, dark, saturated, muted, monochrome and material-led fields. White is a decision, not a fallback. Respect exact color locks and test foregrounds against the actual rendered surface.
7. **Build a grammar, not a costume.** Repeat a few meaningful relationships while changing their expression with the content. Cohesion does not require identical sections; diversity does not require unrelated decoration.
8. **Make interaction carry the idea.** Useful manipulation, exploration and feedback can establish identity. New spatial models need visible cues, labels and keyboard/touch equivalents. Do not demand adaptation through hidden actions, scroll traps, delays or broken conventions.
9. **Let precision supply drama.** In constrained professional work, use editorial selection, beautiful numerals, strong measure, aligned comparisons and carefully judged rules. Boldness is conviction, not a compulsory bright palette or oversized headline.
10. **Protect the evidence.** Never distort a chart, conceal uncertainty, fake functionality or change required facts to make a design more exciting. The design can challenge taste while making its content easier to understand.

## Decide, Build, Then Challenge

For new or substantial work, explore contrasting directions internally: change the organizing idea, information sequence and spatial relationships, not only tokens. Include a direction beyond the first comfortable solution. Choose the strongest prompt fit without an aesthetic questionnaire; deliver one resolved artifact unless alternatives were requested.

Write a concise voice statement in the existing design record: subject, emotional register, defining move and the deliberate restraint that makes it work. Identify the strongest conventional assumption to challenge within scope. Use relevant capabilities because they express the idea, not to meet a feature quota.

After rendering, ask: Could another product or document use this unchanged after swapping the title and accent? Is the defining move visible without the accompanying explanation? Do type, space, surfaces, graphics and behavior speak the same language? For professional output, inspect the precision and hierarchy at reading size; it need not look like a poster in thumbnail.

If the result is interchangeable, incoherent or merely decorated, change the organizing relationship and render again. Do not mark distinction passed for “clean enough.” If rendering or essential assets are unavailable, report incomplete evidence rather than inventing a successful review. Never claim that compliance with this process guarantees a human reaction.

## Use the Established Framework

This creative standard operates inside [Dazzler's design principles](design-philosophy.md) and [deslop framework](deslop.md). It is not a second theme engine, token system or styling layer. Choose expressive relationships with the existing building blocks, persist them in the existing design record, and validate the existing output path.

| Design decision | Existing building block | Established boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type voice and dramatic hierarchy | Font catalog/helper, typography roles, fluid scales | Real files, coverage, licenses, reading measure and heading policy |
| Surface, mood and visual contrast | Color engine, semantic roles and locks | Exact brand locks and measured rendered contrast |
| Spatial tension and rhythm | Composition/refinement, authored page composition, project framework | Semantic order, responsive reflow, content and scope |
| Charts and original visual explanations | Visualization and illustration helpers | Truthful encoding, exact data, labels and text equivalents |
| Word/PDF and slide character | Document directions, native styles, page systems and exporter | Editability claims, font verification and actual page renders |
| Dynamic behavior | Existing native/framework components and motion controls | Working controls, performance, keyboard access and reduced motion |
| Continuity and review | Persistent design record, workflow evidence and delivery gates | Honest observations, retained user choices and explicit unresolved checks |

The `variance`, `density` and `motion` controls are implementation choices, not quality scores. Do not set all to 10 as a substitute for art direction. A `quieter` request can reduce visual intensity while retaining exceptional character; existing explicit values and saved systems remain authoritative. Use custom implementation only when the established components cannot express the chosen idea, and keep its tokens and checks in the same framework.

## Activate the Useful Capabilities

- **Typography:** shortlist against actual copy; export and load the selected local files with their notices. Use genuine weights/italics and numeric features. A font family in JSON is not a loaded font. Give display, reading and numeric roles deliberate treatment; they may share a family. Test long titles and narrow screens.
- **Charts:** state the reader's comparison first; select the encoding, units and time context accordingly. Write a supported takeaway, label the important feature near the data, and include relevant benchmarks only when supplied or derived transparently. `visualize.mjs` supports source-anchored `annotations` and labelled `referenceLines` on single-series bar/line/scatter HTML/SVG charts. Use the project's actual font, background and chart colors. Keep exact rows and missing values; never distort a scale for drama.
- **Original visual:** when the subject benefits, build a diagram, map, illustration, image treatment or motif from its actual material. Use local vector tools, supplied licensed imagery or available image generation as appropriate. Do not add decorative blobs as a substitute for a concept or manufacture evidence-bearing imagery.
- **Interaction:** make a useful comparison, selection or explanation tangible. Hover-only effects and theatrical loading are not capability demonstrations. Use real keyboard/touch states and reduced motion.
- **Documents:** art-direct page sequence, opener, running furniture, tables and charts together. Use native semantic styles and honest editable/static labels. Render every page. A sober document can have a strong typographic silhouette without theatrical decoration.

Read only the relevant capability references: [fonts](typography.md), [studio](design-studio.md), [charts](visualization.md), [illustrations](interactive-illustrations.md), [documents](document-design.md). There is no feature quota: unused capability is fine; default-looking output despite a request for design is not.

## Review the Actual Output

Compare renders with the original or a same-content baseline at thumbnail and reading sizes. Inspect hierarchy, rhythm and subject-specific detail beyond palette. Preserve branding; never impose a Dazzler watermark or house style.

Verify fonts loaded, chart marks/annotations are legible, values reconcile, primary actions work and small-screen/print layouts survive. Record which capabilities actually reached the output and link to concrete elements or captures in the project's existing design record. Do not claim aesthetic superiority from helper calls, feature counts, pixel differences or automated checks. Blind same-brief comparisons require human judgments of fit, distinctiveness and clarity as well as correctness.

## Further Research: Conviction With Coherence

- [Pentagram, The Public Theater](https://www.pentagram.com/work/the-public-theater): the studio describes a changing typographic language tied to productions and the institution. Transfer the relationship between content and visual voice, not its letterforms or layouts.
- [Pentagram, MIT Media Lab](https://www.pentagram.com/work/mit-media-lab): a shared grid supports distinct identities for research groups. Transfer coherent variation, not the grid or glyph designs.
- [Lusion](https://lusion.co/): the studio combines visual storytelling, motion and interaction into an experience. Treat ambitious engineering as a means of expression; do not make heavyweight 3D mandatory.
- [Google's expressive-design research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research): the reported studies connect expressive hierarchy with preference and task visibility, while also documenting usability losses when labels and familiar interaction structures disappear. These findings are context-dependent, not evidence that everyone loves being forced to adapt or that Dazzler guarantees delight.

These sources informed the principles; no artwork or implementation was copied. The desired ambition is a product policy. The source studies do not establish Dazzler's effectiveness.
